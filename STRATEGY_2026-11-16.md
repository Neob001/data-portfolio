# factpipe strategy review: beyond jobs (2026-11-16)

Data: public Apify Store API snapshot of 13,189 Actors with users (2026-11-16), our Console revenue, three research passes (channels, non-jobs public-data markets, jobs-derived products), Store search ranks for 26 buyer queries, a sample of 8 Shopify stores' terms.

## 1. Where we stand

- Revenue Nov 1-16: $74.15, of which Jobs Feed $72.52 (98%). 25 of our 32 Actors are NOT jobs; they have ~0 users.
- Why they failed: they sit in tiny demand pools. Users (30d) across the whole Store: weather 391, grants/tenders 383, patents 97, Wikipedia 193, tech stack 931 — vs jobs ~89,000. And they were thin wrappers over free APIs: no reason to pay us.
- Why jobs worked: an aggregated nightly index (19,000+ sources) nobody can cheaply rebuild, served from free GitHub compute at ~99% margin.
- Our legal rules exclude ~87% of Store demand (social media, Google properties, LinkedIn, Amazon, marketplaces). Most of the legal remainder is Apify's own free tools.
- Ranking: our titles already contain the exact queries, yet `greenhouse jobs`, `workday jobs`, `personio jobs` etc. don't rank us in the top 50. Store search (and agents' MCP `search-actors`) follows usage + quality score, not text. New standalone Actors start at ~0 and stay there unless traffic is routed to them.
- Headroom inside jobs is still 10x: legal leader fantastic-jobs/career-site-job-listing-api has 1,568 users (30d) at $12/1k; we have 160 at $5.

Channels outside the Store:
- **Already live:** agent payments (all our Actors accept AI agents paying via Apify; nothing to set up).
- **AWS Data Exchange:** the only channel with proven bulk buyers for job data (Techmap sells per country per year), 3% fee. But it needs one AWS onboarding support case, a stated support contact, tax/bank forms and S3 storage (cents/month at our size). This conflicts with "zero human contact" and needs an owner decision.
- **MCP registries:** discovery only (Smithery, Glama, official registry pointing at Apify's hosted MCP); low effort, low-medium upside.
- **No fit:** RapidAPI/Zyla (they need our own server, and runs would count against our free credit); Datarade/Snowflake/Databricks (sales contact); Zapier/Make/n8n (no payout); Gumroad (manual support).

## 3. The flywheel: one index factory, one company graph

1. **Index factory (shared code):**
   - discovery (Common Crawl, CT logs, open-data catalogs) → robots.txt + ToS gate → nightly build on free GitHub Actions → static release → delivery Actor template (search / feed / bulk / checker) → auto README, examples, scorecard;
   - every new index reuses ~80% of the jobs code.
2. **Company graph keyed by domain:**
   - jobs (hiring, departments, tech named in posts, salaries) + e-commerce stores (catalog, prices) + tenders (contracts won) + permits (construction activity) all attach to the same company domain;
   - each new index makes every company record richer, which feeds a "company signals by domain" product for the lead-generation category (6x the size of jobs).
3. **History compounds:**
   - daily snapshots turn every index into time-series products (surges, expansions, freezes) and, after 1–2 years, into investor datasets competitors can't backfill.
4. **Traffic routing:**
   - new products launch as output modes or cross-links of the Actor that already has users (Jobs Feed: 160/month), never as isolated Actors;
   - that is how they escape the 0-user ranking trap.
5. **Every product, every channel:**
   - the same files serve the Store, AI agents and (if approved) AWS Data Exchange bulk buyers.

## 4. Priorities (expected return per hour)

1. **Now, no approval needed:**
   - P4 price (scheduled 11-23);
   - JazzHR/Pinpoint rollout (scheduled 11-17);
   - start archiving daily per-company snapshots (internal; unlocks C and the investor dataset; after the 11-17 build is verified).
2. **Approve: agent-readiness pass on the jobs Actors.** Output schemas, a "For AI agents" README section with a spend-capped API example, congruent title/description/schema terms. These are quality-score factors for Store search and MCP agent search. Hours.
3. **Approve: technographics (B)** as a `mode: companies-by-technology` of Jobs Feed (existing 160 users) plus a standalone Actor; ~$15/1k companies (competitors $13–20). Days.
4. **Approve: global tenders index (E)** replacing eu-ted-tenders-monitor + sam-gov-contracts. Clear licences, proven off-Store prices, breadth moat. ~1 week.
5. **Decide: e-commerce store index (D).** Strongest non-jobs Store demand, but only legal under our rules with a per-store terms check (stores that ban crawling are skipped, like robots.txt). Source screen first, then APPROVE/REJECT.
6. **Decide: AWS Data Exchange.** Raises the revenue ceiling most (bulk buyers pay thousands per year), at the cost of one onboarding contact, a support e-mail with auto-reply and cents of S3.
7. **Later:** hiring-change signals (C) after 3 weeks of snapshots; building permits (F) after tenders proves the open-data pattern; MCP registry listings.
8. **Don't:** more per-ATS Actors (ours sit at 0–1 users after 38 days); more thin single-API wrappers; any source in the reject list.
